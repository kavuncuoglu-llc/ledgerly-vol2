Open a customer and choose **Edit** to change their details. Some changes only affect future invoices, so it helps to know which.

## Contact and address

Name, address and contacts can be edited at any time. Existing invoices keep the address they were created with. The pay page and PDFs show the address as it was when the invoice was sent, but a change to a draft takes effect straight away. To correct a sent invoice, see [Edit a sent invoice](/help/invoices/edit-a-sent-invoice).

## Defaults for new invoices

These settings pre-fill new invoices for the customer, overriding the workspace defaults:

- **Payment terms** (for example Net 14 instead of the workspace's Net 30). See [Payment terms](/help/invoices/payment-terms).
- **Currency**, on Pro and Enterprise. A customer can have one default currency.
- **Language** for the invoice and reminder emails.
- **Tax rate** and **Tax exempt** status (see [Tax-exempt customers](/help/taxes/tax-exempt-customers)).

Changing a default never changes invoices that already exist.

## Reminders and late fees

The customer record has a Reminders card for pausing automatic reminders (see [Pause reminders for a customer](/help/reminders/pause-reminders-for-a-customer)) and a **Never charge late fees** switch. The switch is a customer-level exemption and takes effect on existing and future invoices (see [Late fee settings](/help/billing/late-fee-settings)).

## Portal access

Turn on **Customer portal** to let the customer see all their open invoices in one place (see [Customer portal](/help/customers/customer-portal)).

## History

Every change is written to the customer's **Activity** log with the name of the teammate who made it and the time. You cannot delete log entries.

## Who can edit

Owners, Admins and Billers. Viewers can see customers but not change them (see [Team roles and permissions](/help/account/team-roles-and-permissions)).

## Renaming

If the customer changes their company name, edit the name rather than creating a new customer, to keep the history together.
