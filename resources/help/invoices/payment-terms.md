Payment terms tell your client how long they have to pay. Ledgerly turns the term into a due date and uses that date for the Overdue status, reminders and late fees.

## Available terms

| Term | Due date |
| --- | --- |
| Due on receipt | The issue date |
| Net 7 | Issue date + 7 days |
| Net 14 | Issue date + 14 days |
| Net 30 | Issue date + 30 days (the default) |
| Net 45 | Issue date + 45 days |
| Net 60 | Issue date + 60 days |
| Custom | Issue date + 1 to 120 days |

Days are calendar days, including weekends. An invoice issued on March 3 with Net 30 is due on April 2.

## Setting the default

Go to **Settings > Invoices > Defaults** and change **Default payment terms**. It applies to new invoices only. You can also set terms per customer on the customer record, which overrides the workspace default (see [Edit customer details](/help/customers/edit-customer-details)).

## Changing the terms on one invoice

On a draft, pick another term and the due date updates. On a sent invoice you can change the due date directly (see [Edit a sent invoice](/help/invoices/edit-a-sent-invoice)).

## Terms and the rest of the clock

The term decides when the invoice becomes Overdue, which is the day after the due date. Reminders then follow at 3, 7 and 14 days past due, and a late fee is applied 30 days past due. So with Net 30 terms, an unpaid invoice gets a fee about 60 days after it was issued. See [Late fees](/help/billing/late-fees) and [Payment reminders](/help/billing/reminders).

With Due on receipt, the due date and the issue date are the same day, and the invoice turns Overdue on the following day.

## Recurring invoices

Each invoice generated by a schedule gets its due date from the terms on the template, counted from the run date. See [Recurring invoices](/help/recurring/recurring).

For the difference between the two dates on an invoice, see [Due date and issue date](/help/invoices/due-date-vs-issue-date).
