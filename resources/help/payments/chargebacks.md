A chargeback happens when your client asks their bank to reverse a card payment. The bank takes the money back from your LedgerlyPay balance while the case is reviewed.

## What Ledgerly does

When a chargeback is opened:

1. The amount, plus a $15.00 chargeback fee, is deducted from your balance or next payout.
2. The invoice goes back to Sent or Overdue with a note that links to the chargeback.
3. You receive an email and an in-app alert with a response deadline, usually 7 days.
4. Reminders and late fees are paused until the case is closed.

## Responding

1. Open **Payments > Chargebacks** and choose the case.
2. Upload evidence: the signed quote or contract, the invoice PDF, delivery proof and messages with the client.
3. Choose **Submit evidence** before the deadline. A case with no response is lost automatically.

The bank decides within about 30 to 75 days. If you win, the amount is returned to your balance. The $15.00 fee is not returned.

## If you lose

The money stays with the client. The invoice remains unpaid, so you can pursue it outside the card network, record a manual adjustment or issue a [credit note](/help/invoices/credit-notes).

## Reducing the chance of chargebacks

- Make sure your business name on invoices matches what appears on card statements.
- Answer questions from clients quickly.
- Offer a clear [refund](/help/payments/refunds) path.

## Chargeback, refund or dispute

| Term | Started by | Handled in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refund | You | [Refunds](/help/payments/refunds) |
| Dispute | Client, inside Ledgerly | [Disputing a charge](/help/billing/disputes) |
| Chargeback | Client's bank | This article |

A Ledgerly dispute does not involve the bank, and encouraging clients to use it first often avoids a chargeback. ACH payments cannot be charged back in the same way; they can be returned (see [ACH bank transfers](/help/payments/ach-bank-transfers)).
