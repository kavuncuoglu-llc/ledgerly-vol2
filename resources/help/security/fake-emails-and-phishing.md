Scammers sometimes send emails that look like they come from Ledgerly, or send fake invoices that look like they were created with it. A few habits make these easy to spot.

## What Ledgerly will never ask

- For your password, two-factor code or recovery codes
- For a full card number by email or chat
- To pay a Ledgerly invoice to a different bank account than the card on file
- To install software or share your screen

## Signs of a fake

- The sender is not one of our documented addresses: `support@example.com`, `security@example.com`, `team@example.com`, `notify@example.com` or `invoices@example.com`.
- The link text says Ledgerly but the real destination, shown when you hover, is different.
- The message creates urgency, for example "your workspace will be deleted in one hour".
- The invoice number or amount is unfamiliar, or the bank details differ from earlier invoices from the same supplier.

Real Ledgerly emails about your account address you by name and refer to a workspace you own.

## What to do

1. Do not click links or open attachments.
2. Forward the message as an attachment to `security@example.com`.
3. Delete it.
4. If you already clicked and entered details, change your password right away and sign out all sessions (see [Reset your password](/help/security/reset-password) and [Active sessions](/help/security/active-sessions)).
5. If you entered card details, tell your bank.

## Suspicious invoices from your clients' side

If one of your clients receives a message that looks like it came from you with changed bank details, ask them to call you before paying. LedgerlyPay never sends payment instructions by chat.

## Reporting a phishing page

Send the URL to `security@example.com` with a screenshot.
