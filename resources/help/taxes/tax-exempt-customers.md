title: Tax-exempt customers

summary: Shows how to mark a customer as tax exempt so new invoices for them carry no tax.

Some customers, such as non-profits or resellers, do not pay tax on your invoices. You can mark them as exempt so you do not have to remove tax from every line by hand.

## Mark a customer exempt

1. Open the customer and choose **Edit**.
2. Under **Tax**, switch on **Tax exempt**.
3. Enter the reason (for example "Registered charity") and the exemption certificate number, if you have one.
4. Save.

From then on, new invoices for that customer start with no tax rate on their lines, and the invoice prints the reason and certificate number under the totals. You can still add a rate to a particular line if needed.

## What does not change

- Invoices that were already created keep their tax. Edit the lines of a draft, or void and re-issue a sent invoice.
- Recurring templates already created for the customer keep their rates. Update them after marking the customer exempt (see [Edit a recurring template](/help/recurring/edit-a-recurring-template)).

## Documents to keep

Ledgerly stores the certificate number but does not verify it. Keep a copy of the certificate in your records, since tax authorities may ask for it. You can attach a file to the customer record (up to 10 MB).

## Exempt compared with reverse charge

Tax-exempt means no tax is charged and the customer is not required to account for it. The reverse charge is different. It applies to cross-border sales between businesses, where the buyer accounts for the tax. You choose it at invoice level (see [VAT reverse charge](/help/taxes/vat-reverse-charge)).

## Reports

Exempt sales appear in the [tax summary report](/help/taxes/tax-summary-report) under "Exempt" with the sales total but no tax amount.

## Late fees

Late fees are not taxed for any customer (see [Tax on late fees](/help/taxes/tax-on-late-fees)).
